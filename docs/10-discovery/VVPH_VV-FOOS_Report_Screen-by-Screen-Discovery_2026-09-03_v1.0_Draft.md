# VVPH VV-FOOS Report: Screen-by-Screen Discovery, 2026-09-03, v1.0 Draft

## 1. What this is

A sweep of every screen the application can render, captured and measured rather than reviewed by eye. It exists to answer one question: what should be reworked, improved or optimised, with evidence attached to each answer.

27 screens, every route in `App.tsx` and in all five module route trees, including the two placeholder modules and the not-found route. Each captured twice, at 1440x900 and at 390x844, full page. **54 screenshots**, written to `discovery/` at the repository root.

`discovery/` is gitignored on purpose. The images are binary, regenerable in about ninety seconds, and they render seeded candidate names. The measurements they were taken alongside are in `discovery/findings.json`, and the conclusions are in this document.

To regenerate: the sweep lived in a throwaway spec rather than the committed suite, because a screenshot corpus is a research artefact and not a gate. The measurements it collects per screen are DOM node count, interactive control count, `aria-disabled` placeholder count, record-section and table count, `h1` count, document title, and sideways-scroll amount, plus a full axe run at AA and every console error.

## 2. What was already right

Worth stating plainly, because it is most of the app.

- **No console errors or page errors on any of the 27 screens.**
- **No sideways scroll on any of the 27 screens at 390px.** Q8 was verified on seven routes; this confirms it on all of them.
- **Exactly one `h1` per screen**, on all 27.
- **No axe violation at serious or critical impact on 26 of 27**, after the two this sweep found were fixed.

## 3. What the sweep found

### 3.1 A critical accessibility defect, fixed

`/?tab=pipeline` carried **`select-name` at critical impact**: the dashboard's date-range `<select>` had no accessible name at all, so a screen reader announced a combo box with no indication of what it changed. Its two custom-range date inputs had the same problem. All three now carry `aria-label`.

The reason it survived until now is the more useful finding: **the axe test covered seven routes and the app has 27.** `/` was covered, `/?tab=pipeline` was not, and the pipeline tab is a different widget tree. The route list is now **17 routes**, adding the pipeline tab, global search, all four "new" forms, contacts, tickets, promote and both intake sub-pages.

### 3.2 A serious contrast defect, fixed

The dashboard KPI labels measured **3.59:1**, below the 4.5:1 AA threshold. The cause is worth recording because the contrast guard did not catch it: `--fo-text-secondary` (`#5f5f5f`) is fine on all three grounds the guard checks, 5.60:1 at worst, but the KPI cards are painted on the **section tint** `#a9c6dc`, which the guard had no pair for. The label now uses `--fo-tab-label` (`#4a4a4a`), 4.98:1 on that tint, which is an existing token the guard already checks rather than a new one.

The durable fix is the axe coverage in 3.1 rather than another token pair, because axe computes the actual rendered pair on every element and needs nobody to have thought of the combination in advance.

### 3.3 Every screen had the same document title, fixed

All 27 screens reported `VentVest Front Office OS`. In an application that ships a **bookmark bar**, that means every bookmark, every browser-history entry and every open tab is indistinguishable. `ListPage` and `RecordPage` already receive a `title`, so a `useDocumentTitle` hook in both was enough; a record page includes its display code, so a bookmarked requisition reads `REQ-0001 Senior Data Engineer — VentVest Front Office OS`. Two tests hold it, including one asserting no two list screens share a title.

### 3.4 The activity timeline has no cap

`deal-record` renders **4,263 DOM nodes**, against 250 to 500 for its peers. The composition explains it: **362 timeline items**, and because each carries an icon, 365 `<svg>` and **1,456 `<path>` elements**. The timeline renders every activity ever recorded against the record, unbounded.

Two honest qualifications. First, 361 of those 362 activities are `status_change` rows generated by this project's own e2e suite moving that seeded deal's stage over many runs, so the volume is test residue rather than real history. Second, the defect is real regardless: nothing caps the list, so a genuine deal with two years of history renders the same way. Recorded as **T2** rather than fixed here, because the cap is a product decision (how many, and what the "show earlier" affordance is) rather than a bug with one right answer.

It also tells us something about the suite: 1,844 activity rows now exist in a database seeded with 10 people. Trigger-written rows are append-only by design, as `audit_log` is, so this is accepted practice here, but it will skew any future measurement taken against seeded data.

### 3.5 The unimplemented surface, quantified

**175 controls across 27 screens are `aria-disabled` placeholders**, routed through `TaleoPlaceholder` as the fidelity work requires. That is the honest size of the "looks like Taleo, does not do it yet" surface, and it is now a number rather than an impression. The heaviest are the two placeholder modules (11 and 8), the requisition form and its edit view (12 each), the candidate record (12) and the dashboard (16).

Nothing to fix. It is worth having the figure in front of anyone deciding what to build next: the screens that look most complete are not the ones with the fewest placeholders.

| T5 | Keep the axe route list in step with the router | The list covered 7 of 27 routes and missed a critical violation | Consider generating the list from the route tree so a new route cannot be added without coverage |

## 6. What this sweep deliberately did not do

It measured structure, accessibility, errors and responsive behaviour. It did **not** judge visual fidelity against Taleo, because that is `docs/02-taleo-fidelity/` and needs the screenshots put side by side with the originals by someone who can see both. The 54 images exist for exactly that purpose.

It also did not measure authenticated performance per screen. That is K10, which needs a deployed instance rather than loopback.
