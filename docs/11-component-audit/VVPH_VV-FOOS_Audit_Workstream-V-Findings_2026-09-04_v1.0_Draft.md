# Workstream V: the audit, and where each part of it lives

Version 1.0. Audit and specification only: nothing here changes a component. Workstream W builds from it, and its brief is `docs/03-plan/VVPH_VV-FOOS_Goal_Workstream-W-Component-Implementation_2026-09-04_v1.0_Draft.md`.

**Every figure in this document set is regenerable.** `node tests/audit/report-figures.mjs` reads the capture coverage, the source counts, the row and severity totals and the document versions from the corpus and from `app/src`, because 23 of the claims corrected during pass 6 were hand-typed numbers that had gone stale.

The screenshot corpus is `audit/corpus/`, **gitignored**, because the screens render real candidate names from the Zoho migration. Each area has its own `manifest.<area>.json` naming component, state and viewport.

**These documents carry no candidate data.** Where a finding needed an example of imported data, the shape is described and the value is not reproduced; V10 observation 14 is the one place that had four real first names in it and they were removed on 2026-09-04. The manifests do contain candidate names, in the `name` field of captures of name links and in file paths, which is a second reason the corpus stays out of git. Anything quoted from a manifest into a document has to be checked for that first.

## The findings that would change what W builds first

1. **The bulk-select header shows the wrong state and can silently widen a bulk action.** `indeterminate` appears nowhere in the codebase. With 3 of 20 rows selected the header renders unchecked, and clicking it replaces the selection with all 20, so a bulk action then applies to twenty records while the user believes it applies to three. Verified in `Table.tsx`. **The audit's only `Blocks` finding and rank 1 in V8**, ahead of the control height scale, because it is a handful of lines, needs no design decision, and meets the second clause of V12's definition: completes it wrongly without knowing.
2. **The keyboard focus ring is invisible on the two dark shell surfaces, and the count of surfaces was wrong.** `outline: 2px solid var(--fo-primary)` at `#3679b5` scores **1.013:1** against the primary nav's `#427d98` and **1.003:1** against the sidebar panel header's `#577995`, where WCAG 1.4.11 requires 3:1, so a keyboard user has no visible position across all 14 nav tabs or the sidebar panel headers. **Corrected on 2026-09-04:** I had listed four surfaces, adding Logout and any hovered primary button. Both were wrong, because `outline-offset: 1px` places the ring outside the element's border box, so the surface it must contrast against is the nearest painted ancestor rather than the control. Logout's ring lands on the white utility header at 4.606:1 and a primary button's on the white page surface at 4.606:1, and both pass. `check-contrast.mjs` line 20 tests the ring against `--fo-surface` only, so the gate is green. The measurement pass now records the painted ancestor of every control and computes the ratio, so V9 will give the definitive list rather than my reading of the cascade.
3. **The select is one component, not fifty-four.** Two `<select>` elements exist; `components/ui/Select.tsx` is used 54 times across 23 files. The complaint resolves to one replacement.
4. **Nothing declares a control height, and the mechanism is now measured.** Only **two rules in the entire product set a `line-height` on a control**, `.btn` at 17.5px and `.btn-sm` at 16.25px, which produce the only two stable heights, 37.5px and 30.25px. Every other control computes `line-height: normal` and takes its height from font metrics plus padding, with a 13.33px user-agent default font recurring, so for most controls nobody chose a height and for several nobody chose a font size. The result is **15 distinct chrome-button heights across a 28px spread**, and a `select` that is 19px or 36px against an `input-text` that is 21, 24, 25 or 34, so the two controls most likely to share a filter row never match. On target size, measured across all 3,948 controls: **643 are under 24px on a side, 615 are rescued by WCAG 2.5.8's spacing exception, and 28 fail outright** — the requisition owners checkbox list, four 13x13 checkboxes stacked 23px apart where 24 is needed, failing by one pixel, and the rich-text toolbar at 390px only. The pager arrows conform and the stars already meet 24 by 24.
5. **A candidate row is 90px tall and the tallest control in it is 18px**, so 72px of every row carries no control, five times over, on the list holding 72,917 records. Both recruit lists exceed Carbon's largest published row size, which is itself reserved for two-line content. The rows also **grew 30% and 33% when the migration landed**, from 69px and 55px measured on 2026-09-01, with no stylesheet change: real names and grade comments wrap where seed data did not. Row height is a function of content here, so capping the wrapping columns is the lever.
6. **Density is a component behaviour, not a spacing token.** Panels keep their full chrome when empty, which is the mechanism behind the 47% empty home page the fidelity audit already called the single most visible departure from Taleo. **The candidate record's height is disputed between two of my own earlier measurements**, 7,304px over 9.71 viewports against the plan's 5,979px in a 572px window, and V13 takes the 900px measurement over both rather than picking one.
7. **`aria-disabled` on 60 of 61 controls that respond** gives screen-reader and sighted users different products. 50 `<PlaceholderAction` renders, none opting out, plus 11 hand-rolled `show(...)` buttons of which 10 also carry it. Exactly one placeholder action does not: `CandidateRecordPage.tsx:290`, the `Flag` button. Corrected three times: 66 counted imports and the definition, and 51 counted a line holding `<PlaceholderActionGear`, a hand-rolled local copy of the whole pattern.
8. **Row-level controls carry no row identity, so 61% of the candidate list's controls are indistinguishable by name.** `Table.tsx:268` labels every row checkbox `Select row`, `StarGrade.tsx:31` labels every star group `Star grade` and names each star from a `title` alone, and `ApplicationPipeline.tsx:428` gives 21 controls the same `Add to shortlist`. Measured: 100 of 163 controls on the candidate list share a name with nineteen others. This compounds finding 1: the header checkbox shows the wrong state *and* no row can be told apart by name.
9. **Twelve of 31 routes set no document title, plus `/login`.** WCAG 2.4.2 is **Level A**, and its Understanding document says an SPA must change the title per view. `useDocumentTitle` already exists and `ListPage` and `RecordPage` call it; the dashboard, global search, both intake importers, all four placeholder modules, both not-found stubs and the login screen call nothing, so all of them read `VentVest Front Office OS`.
10. **Five dialogs declare `aria-modal="true"` and none traps or restores focus.** `IdleSignOut`, `Placeholder`, `recruit/Dialog`, `sales/Modal` and `intake/Drawer` all set it, and grep for `activeElement`, `trapFocus` or `focusTrap` across `app/src` returns nothing, so Tab walks out of every dialog and focus lands on the body on close. Three of the five have no `autoFocus` either. The APG warns the attribute should be set only when application code actually prevents external interaction, because misuse "can prevent users of some assistive technologies from perceiving content outside the dialog", so this asserts an inertness that does not exist. The three module dialogs at 43, 40 and 39 lines are also the same component written three times.
11. **`Back To List` loses the list on 9 of 11 paths, and both halves of the fix are already written.** `lib/listReturn.ts` provides `useListLink` to store the list URL and `useBackToList` to read it back. `useListLink` is used at exactly two call sites, both `onRowClick`, so on the recruit lists clicking the row background preserves the page, filters and sort while clicking the candidate's **name link** does not. On the sales lists neither works, and no sales record page has the button at all. This is the fidelity audit's C13, recorded as absent from FOOS, and it is more precisely half-built.
12. **Four interactions in the product are mouse-only, all Level A.** The sortable column header (`Table.tsx:212`), the truncated-cell expander across five columns (`CappedCell`), every dashboard bar's drill-through (`BarList.tsx:11`), and the deal card's navigation (`DealsListPage.tsx:216`), which gives the sharpest case: a keyboard user can move a deal between pipeline stages but cannot open it. Established by enumerating all 14 `onClick` handlers on non-interactive tags and discarding the 10 that are backdrop closes or `stopPropagation` guards.
13. **No section title in the product is a heading.** `RecordSection` renders its title as bare text inside `<button aria-expanded>`, which is the correct disclosure pattern and stops one step short of the APG's advice to wrap it in a heading. The app has **39 heading elements** against **76** `RecordSection` call sites, and a record page with seventeen collapsible sections measures **2 headings**, with 24 of 31 routes at exactly 1. Jumping by heading, which is how a screen-reader user moves around a long page, does not work anywhere in FOOS.
14. **The loading-feedback pattern exists, sales and intake use it everywhere, and recruit uses none of it.** Five tables gate their empty row on `loading` and seven buttons change their label to `Saving…` or `Exporting…`; **0 of recruit's three lists do either**, and neither recruit list passes `busy`. So the two highest-traffic screens in the ATS show "No candidates match." while the query runs, while the CRM beside them says "Loading…". Separately the pager announces the page position, `1 / 3646`, and not the result count, `1-20 of 72,917`, from two spans six lines apart in one component.

## One decision the audit cannot make

**Is the ATS meant to be read-only on a phone?** V6 row 32: `useIsMobile` gates eight capabilities on a 640px media query, so create, edit, bulk-edit and upload all vanish below that width, in recruit only, while sales allows them at any width. The policy is defensible and may well be intended; it is parked as **P4** in the parked-and-blocked register rather than decided here.

Three parts of it are defects whichever way that goes, and W8 should fix them regardless: nothing tells the user why the controls are absent, the live listener means narrowing a desktop window removes them mid-session, and the module README names the hook without recording what it does.

## What this audit does not know

- **Chromium only.** No Firefox or Safari, which matters for a workstream about browser-decided controls.
- **No error or validation evidence yet.** Row 14 of V6 is the only row still unverified, and pass 3 supplies it.
- **The placeholder modal has never been captured**, because Playwright refuses to click an `aria-disabled` button. Fixed, and a targeted pass is queued.
- **One row is rated Blocks**, row 5, and it took until 2026-09-04 to get there because I had tested only the first half of V12's own definition. Blocks is "the user cannot complete the task, **or completes it wrongly without knowing**", and a bulk action silently applied to twenty records instead of three is the second clause exactly. It is now rank 1 in V8, ahead of the height scale, and it is among the cheapest items in the workstream.
